\chapter{CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Conclusion}

This thesis addressed \textbf{Hospital Chatbot for Color-Coded Clinical Pathways} in two parts. Part~1 delivered a working Digital Acuity Engine: medical gating, optional clinical NER, fine-tuned BioBERT acuity prediction, and deterministic SATS colour mapping, with strong holdout colour-routing performance. Part~2 specified protocol-mapped pathways so each colour carries destination, time target, and actions---including Green diversion and Blue dignity---thereby attacking acuity discontinuity without pretending that a badge alone is a pathway.

We explicitly \textbf{did not} implement Bayesian fusion. Softmax already yields an acuity distribution from text; pathways attach by \(f_{\mathrm{SATS}}\) and \(P(C)\). That answers RQ10 with a lean LMIC intake architecture.

\section{Answers to research questions (summary)}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RQ1--RQ2:} Yes---high holdout acuity/colour accuracy under fixed \(f_{\mathrm{SATS}}\).
  \item \textbf{RQ3, RQ9:} Pathways defined as protocol table including Green diversion.
  \item \textbf{RQ4:} Curatio chatbot/API demonstrates pre-desk delivery of acuity and colour (pathway card as design completion).
  \item \textbf{RQ5--RQ6:} Gate and OpenMed behave as intended in system tests.
  \item \textbf{RQ7--RQ8:} Red recall and calibration overconfidence require safety-aware deployment.
  \item \textbf{RQ10:} NLP\(\to\)colour\(\to\)\(P(C)\) is a defensible intake stack without TEWS--Bayes.
\end{itemize}

\section{Contributions restated}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Implemented and evaluated text-to-SATS-colour triage NLP.
  \item Formalised colour-coded clinical pathway protocols for chatbot output.
  \item Scoped mathematics honestly: softmax + deterministic maps in; Bayesian fusion out.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Future work}

\end{itemize}
