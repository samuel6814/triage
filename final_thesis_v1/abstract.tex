\chapter*{ABSTRACT}

\textbf{Triage} is the structured process of assigning each arriving patient an urgency level so that hospital resources match clinical need. In low- and middle-income countries, including Ghana, overcrowding, delayed first assessment, and \emph{acuity discontinuity} (urgency colour stops guiding care after intake) affect outpatient departments, wards, and emergency streams alike. This project develops a hospital chatbot for color-coded clinical pathways using Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (BERT): it maps unstructured \emph{chief complaints} (unstructured descriptions of why the patient has come) to South African Triage Scale (SATS) colours and clinical pathways.

The mathematical model converts English patient text to a colour through a fine-tuned Biomedical Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (BioBERT) classifier. Tokenisation \(\tau\), three embedding components, multi-head self-attention, and Softmax classification (framed as a Categorical distribution over five mutually exclusive acuity levels) are specified with full formulas. A fixed map \(f_{\mathrm{SATS}}\) converts predicted acuity levels to Red, Orange, Yellow, or Green. The Triage Early Warning Score (TEWS) is presented as the parallel hospital standard for objective vital signs; when vitals are unavailable at intake, the text model supplies the initial colour. A clinical relevance gate filters non-medical input before classification. Pathway cards \(P(C)\) bind each colour to destination, maximum waiting time, and escalation at \examplehospital{}.

Holdout evaluation on the 80{,}000-pair Triagegeist benchmark reports 99.92\% accuracy and approximately 100\,ms median inference latency. Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique (SMOTE) and stratified training mitigate class imbalance, raising Level~1 (Red) recall from 98.14\% to 100\% at a small precision cost, prioritising a safety-first clinical trade-off. The chatbot is positioned as nurse-overridable triage assist, not as a replacement for clinical judgment.

The methodology is illustrated on a running complaint at \examplehospital{}: \runningcomplaint{} System architecture and the prototype \texttt{/predict} application programming interface (API) are documented in Chapter~\ref{ch:method} and Appendix~\ref{app:github}. Results and conclusion are reported in later chapters.
