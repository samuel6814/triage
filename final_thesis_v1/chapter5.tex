\chapter{CONCLUSION}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Summary}

This project developed a mathematical pipeline from unstructured chief complaint text to South African Triage Scale (SATS) colour and clinical pathway at hospital intake. The chatbot accepts a single English free-text submission and returns acuity probabilities, a colour, and a pathway card aligned with SATS and \examplehospital{} department structure.

Methodologically, the work specifies tokenisation \(\tau\), contextual embeddings (word, position, and segment), twelve-layer BioBERT encoding with multi-head self-attention, Softmax classification as a Categorical distribution over five mutually exclusive acuity levels, the deterministic map \(f_{\mathrm{SATS}}\), a clinical relevance gate, and department routing across seventeen hospital units. TEWS is documented as the parallel nurse-facing vital-sign standard, not as a missing chatbot feature. Training is documented as a three-stage stack: PubMed masked language modelling (Stage 1), encoder transfer from a prior triage checkpoint (Stage 2), and comprehensive project fine-tuning on the Triagegeist English benchmark (80{,}000 pairs, Section~\ref{sec:data-provenance}) with a reinitialised five-class head (Stage 3).

Holdout evaluation reports 99.92\% accuracy, 0.9977 macro-F1, and 98.14\% Level 1 recall on 8{,}000 validation complaints, with approximately 100\,ms inference latency (Chapter~\ref{ch:results}, Figures~\ref{fig:latency}--\ref{fig:metrics-compare}). A full five-scenario pathway map (gate reject, Red, Orange, Yellow, Green) routes each NLP outcome to clinical and support departments at \examplehospital{}, addressing intake delay, queue congestion, and acuity discontinuity described in Chapter~\ref{ch:intro}.

\section{Answers to research questions}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{RQ1--RQ2.} Yes on in-distribution Triagegeist English text: holdout acuity accuracy is 99.92\%, and colour follows immediately from fixed \(f_{\mathrm{SATS}}\) once \(\hat{c}\) is known.
\item \textbf{RQ3.} Pathway cards \(P(C)\) are specified as \((T_{\max},\,\text{destination},\,\text{actions},\,\text{escalation})\) with complete scenario tables and a seventeen-department routing map (Section~\ref{sec:pathways}).
\item \textbf{RQ4.} The Curatio \texttt{/predict} prototype returns colour and pathway from text alone in about 100\,ms; nurse TEWS continues in parallel when vitals exist.
\item \textbf{RQ5.} The gate with threshold \(0.35\) rejects greeting-only and non-clinical input before BioBERT, as illustrated in Section~\ref{sec:gate}.
\item \textbf{RQ6.} Level~1 is the minority class; baseline Red recall is 98.14\%, and SMOTE raises it to 100\% at a small cost in overall accuracy and Red precision (Chapter~\ref{ch:results}).
\item \textbf{RQ7.} Softmax confidence should trigger nurse oversight when the mode is not dominant (near ties) or when language conflicts with vitals; Softmax is a Categorical assist, not a clinical certificate.
\item \textbf{RQ8.} Green pathways divert routine presentations to outpatient and specialty streams within four hours, reducing acute congestion while preserving nurse override.
\end{itemize}

\section{Contributions restated}

\begin{enumerate}
\item An examinable text-to-SATS-colour mathematical pipeline using BioBERT and Categorical Softmax.
\item Measured Stage~3 fine-tuning results (baseline and SMOTE) with latency suitable for intake assist.
\item Protocol-mapped colour-coded clinical pathways for \examplehospital{}, including Green diversion and gate rejection.
\item A deployable chatbot API with medical gating, documented alongside TEWS as the vital-sign parallel path.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations and future work}

\textbf{Data scope.} Reported holdout metrics come exclusively from the Triagegeist synthetic English benchmark (Section~\ref{sec:data-provenance}); the model is English-only at intake. A site visit to \examplehospital{} informed pathway design and generated supplementary English example complaints for prototype and qualitative analysis, but those examples were not used for fine-tuning. A natural next step is prospective collection of nurse-labeled chief complaints at \examplehospital{} for local retraining, and exploring optional fusion of vitals and TEWS alongside free text when measurements become available.

\textbf{Class imbalance.} Chapter~\ref{ch:results} already evaluates baseline and SMOTE-augmented checkpoints with stratified holdout metrics. Future work can refine minority-class sensitivity for urgent levels while preserving specificity on routine presentations, building on the imbalance-mitigation pipeline documented in Section~\ref{sec:finetuned-analysis}.

\textbf{Clinical validation.} Holdout evaluation establishes strong offline agreement with nurse labels. The next phase is a prospective pilot at \examplehospital{} to measure waiting times, nurse override rates, and patient outcomes in live intake, converting offline performance into operational evidence \citep{Mitchell2023}.

\textbf{Pathway refinement.} The five-scenario pathway map is SATS-aligned and complete for gate reject, Red, Orange, Yellow, and Green routing. Ongoing collaboration with hospital administrators and clinical leads at \examplehospital{} will keep department routing tables current as service lines evolve \citep{Rominski2014,satsmanual}.

\textbf{Language and equity.} Extension to Ghanaian informal English and optional Twi-assisted intake would reduce exclusion of patients who do not write formal English complaints.

\textbf{System integration.} The prototype \texttt{/predict} API and pathway cards already anchor the chatbot in a deployable pipeline. Deeper integration with Hospital Information Technology and Health Information systems would automate timer alerts and bed assignment, completing the Flow Management Engine vision across Administration, Diagnostics, and all clinical departments listed in Table~\ref{tab:knust-dept-routing}.
